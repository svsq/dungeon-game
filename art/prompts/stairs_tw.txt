Use case: the way down into the crypt in the start town of a top-down 3/4 dungeon game, built INTO the fortress wall. Image 1 is the style reference. Image 2 is the approved town concept's crypt stairs crop: copy its design closely (a dark stairwell opening set in a thick wall of stacked slate blocks, steps going down into darkness, ivy hanging from the top), NOT its warm light. Image 3 is this game's own wall texture: the masonry of this prop MUST be the same stone: same block size, same colours, same dark mortar, so the prop melts into a wall built of that texture.
Subject: seen from the 3/4 view, front face visible: a section of ruined fortress wall of STACKED, FLAT, ANGULAR SLATE BLOCKS with a tall square-topped doorway in its middle, framed by two thick block jambs and a heavy stone lintel. Through the doorway a straight flight of about seven stone steps goes DOWN and away into the dark, every step readable, each darker than the one above, the far end pure black. The wall's outer left and right edges and its top are ragged, stepped block by block, so it can overlap a longer wall without a straight seam. Moss on the top blocks, ivy hanging from the lintel on one side.
Materials: slate with a faint violet-brown tint (#2a2128, #372b34, #4b3943, #5f464f, top edge #81676f), dark mortar lines; not lighter than the texture in Image 3.
View and scale: the 3/4 view of Image 1; the whole piece is 46 art pixels wide and 36 tall, the doorway 16 wide and 22 tall (the hero is 26, a barrel 14).
Composition: one prop centred, complete, wide empty margin, transparent background.
Constraints: painted UNLIT: no warm glow, no braziers, no torches, no light spill, no figures, no ground plane, no shadow, no text.
