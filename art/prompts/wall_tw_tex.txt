Use case: a seamless tileable wall-face material for the ruined fortress of the start town of a top-down 3/4 dungeon game. Image 1 is the style reference. Image 2 is the approved town concept's crop of its ruined walls: copy its broken stone, NOT its warm light.
Subject: seen straight on, MANY small flat angular slate blocks in broken irregular courses, each block only 12 to 18 art pixels wide and 7 to 11 art pixels tall, of uneven width and uneven height so no joint lines up across three courses and no pattern repeats. Each block has a pale top edge one art pixel tall (#81676f) over a mid face (#4b3943, #5f464f, #372b34) and a dark lower edge (#2a2128). Blocks are separated by thin near-black mortar gaps one art pixel wide (#141012). Many corners chipped and broken off, some blocks cracked, a few blocks clearly darker or lighter than their neighbours, and small sparse patches of dark moss (#302b09, #473e0b) in a few joints. Faint warm violet-brown slate, hue 320 to 345, the same stone as the fortress gate; never blue, never lavender, never pink or salmon.
Composition: the whole canvas is wall, edge to edge, tiling seamlessly in both directions, 128x128 art pixels, each art pixel an 8x8 block of one colour. Constraints: flat even light, no gradient, no glow, no objects, no text.
