Use case: a floor spike-trap plate for a top-down 3/4 dungeon game, the RETRACTED state (spikes down, but their tips still show, so the player can always tell it is a trap). Image 1 is the style reference only: match its iron, stone floor and lighting. Image 2 is the dungeon floor stone this plate sits in: the plate's rim must be that same stone colour.
Subject: a square dark iron plate sunk into the dungeon floor, seen from directly above like the floor of Image 1 (NOT isometric, NOT a diamond: a plain square with horizontal and vertical edges). No separate frame: the outer rim is a thin worn border of the same warm grey-brown stone as Image 2, then the riveted dark iron plate. On the iron, FOUR small clusters of holes in a 2x2 layout, the cluster centres at about 30% and 70% of the plate width and height; each cluster is 3 small round holes close together (a triangle). Inside EVERY hole a dull steel spike tip points up: a small light grey-cream point with a dark outline, clearly visible. Holes are dark brown-grey inside, not pure black, and small: each hole about 1/16 of the plate wide. Worn dark iron between them, a few rivets, a little old dried blood and scratches near the clusters.
Composition: one square plate filling about 90% of the canvas, centred, its edges parallel to the canvas edges. The plate is 32x32 art pixels, every art pixel drawn as a block of one colour, with a 1-pixel dark outline around the iron and around each hole.
Constraints: it must NOT look like a window, a grate or a hatch: no big dark squares, no bars, no frame. No shadow, no floor around it, no glow.
