One isolated floor piece for a top-down 3/4 town map, seen from DIRECTLY ABOVE, lying flat on the ground: a straight stretch of narrow-gauge mine track running horizontally across the image, exactly nine sleepers, evenly spaced and close together: the gap between two sleepers is only about as wide as one sleeper, as on a real mine track. Under it a bed of ballast: a flat strip of small angular broken grey slate stones and grit (#252534, #3e3234, #6b5c56, a few #81676f tops), a little wider than the sleepers are long, its long edges ragged where the stones thin out onto bare earth. The sleepers are short thick timbers of dark weathered wood (#281510, #3f261c, #743f1d, worn edge #a86d32) lying across the track, each MUCH longer than the gauge: they reach well out past both rails, about one rail gap beyond each rail. Two thin dark iron rails (#2a2128, #4b3943, a cold steel top line #8a8a96) run straight and perfectly parallel the whole width of the image over all the sleepers, fixed by small dark spikes. Ballast grit fills the gaps between the sleepers. The design follows Image 2 (a crop of the approved town concept, its rails on wooden sleepers). Chunky pixel-art style of Image 1, 1-pixel dark outline (#060506) on the sleepers and rails only, 3-4 hard tone steps per material, no gradients, no anti-aliasing. Painted UNLIT: even neutral light, no glow, no cast shadow. No cart, no grass, no text. Transparent background around the ballast strip, with margins above and below.
