Use case: a seamless tileable ground material for the start town of a top-down 3/4 dungeon game, a ruined fortress courtyard taken back by nature (Image 1 is the style reference for pixel size, line and shading; Image 2 is the approved town concept's ground crop: take only its ground material and palette, never its warm firelight).
Subject: dark umber earth seen straight from above, a cool dark brown, never red or rust (#1c1712, #2a1d17, #3a2a1f, #432e22), packed and uneven with visible material in mid tones (#4a3527, #57402f): many small clods, a dense scatter of tiny grey-brown pebbles (#5c4d44, #6e5d52, 2-4 art pixels), irregular darker and paler soil patches 10-24 art pixels wide with broken edges, faint cracks; grass appears ONLY as many small SEPARATE tufts (each tuft 3-6 art pixels, olive #302b09, #473e0b, #675a0d) spread evenly and sparsely over the whole surface, with bare earth between them; no large grass patches, no moss carpets, no big features of any kind, so that the repeat of the tile cannot be seen.
Composition: the whole canvas is ground, edge to edge. It must tile seamlessly in both directions. The canvas is exactly 256x256 art pixels, every art pixel drawn as a 4x4 block of one colour; even density everywhere.
Constraints: flat even lighting, no vignette, no gradient across the image, no warm light pools, no flowers, no objects, no paths, no walls, no large shapes.
