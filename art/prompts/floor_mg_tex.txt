Use case: a seamless tileable floor material for the magma caves' plateaus in a top-down 3/4 dungeon game (Image 1 is the style reference for pixel size, line and shading; Image 2 is a crop of the approved magma cave concept: take only the walkable ash ground with its cracked plates and its palette, not its lava or its light).
Subject: volcanic plateau ground seen straight from above, no masonry: a mid charcoal-grey bed of packed ash and fine basalt grit, broken by flat cracked plates of darker stone 20 to 30 art pixels across with straight angular edges and sharp corners (never rounded, never rectangular, never in rows or a grid), each plate outlined by a 1-pixel near-black crack, the cracks joining into a loose network; small dark basalt crumbs (1 to 3 art pixels) spread evenly over the ash, no clusters. Every plate and every crumb is DARKER than the ash bed: no lighter blobs, no pale patches, no cloud- or lichen-shaped spots, nothing that stands out as a landmark when the tile repeats. Neutral near-black and charcoal grey (#141314, #232122, #34302f, #4a4544), never brown, never orange; a faint dark red (#4f150c) deep inside a few cracks only.
Composition: the whole canvas is ground, edge to edge, evenly busy everywhere. It must tile seamlessly in both directions. The canvas is exactly 128x128 art pixels, every art pixel drawn as an 8x8 block of one colour.
Constraints: flat even neutral lighting with no vignette, no gradient across the image, no glow, no lava, no fire, no bright orange, no bricks, no slabs in a grid, no mortar lines, no objects, no bones, no props, no walls.
